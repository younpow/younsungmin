OUR TIME observes the time accumulated through ordinary days rather than extraordinary events.

Returning to the same domestic space, I photograph small changes: objects shift, light moves across the room, weather changes beyond the window, and the child gradually changes as well. In some photographs the child is present; in others, absent. Even without a figure, traces of the time spent together remain in the space.

The time in these photographs does not belong to the child alone. It is our time—the time shared by those who have lived together within this space.

Developed through repeated observation over seasons and years, OUR TIME considers how shared time passes and accumulates at once, leaving subtle traces in people, objects, and everyday spaces.
